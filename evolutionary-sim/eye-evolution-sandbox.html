Eyes evolving in a world, not on a fitness curve

No survival formula is written down here, and there is no food and no energy score. Prey and predators start with whatever eye you dial in (blind by default) and move around a 2D sea. The only thing an eye does is let you detect things farther away (a better eye sees farther). Prey simply breed on a timer as long as they stay alive; a predator must catch prey to reset its starvation clock or it dies. Everything reduces to who sees whom first: a predator that spots prey while still unseen closes in (it's 20% faster); a prey that spots the predator first slips away while the predator is still blind. Watch both eyes sharpen in an arms race driven by nothing but survival — and use the controls to find the conditions where they don't.

The arms race — mean eye blur Δρ over time

Lower = sharper eye. Both lines should fall as eyes evolve; the predator line chasing the prey line down (or vice-versa) is the arms race. Flat/high = still blind.

Does a sharper eye actually leave more descendants?

Every dot is one animal, positioned by the optical blur it was born with. Filled dots have died; hollow dots are still alive, so their lifetimes are only lower bounds. The pale line is the mean of ended animals within each resolution band—not a fitted fitness curve.

What is and isn't authored here (read me)

Authored (the substrate): the optics equation Δρ=√((A/f·(1−lens))²+(λ/A)²) shared with the other two tabs; that a sharper eye sees farther (range = acuity × best-eye range); fixed movement speeds (predator 20% faster); the starvation clock and breeding timer; and the hard-coded behaviors — prey flee the nearest visible predator else wander; predators pursue the nearest visible prey else wander. Evolvable brains that would let behavior itself emerge are a later step.

NOT authored (it emerges): who survives, who breeds, whether an eye is "worth it," and how sharp each side's eye becomes. There is no survival-probability curve and no fitness function. A prey lives because it happened to spot a predator far enough away to slip out of sight before contact, and breeds because it stayed alive long enough for its timer; a predator lives because it caught prey before its clock ran out. Because the predator is faster, a tie in detection range goes to the predator — so prey are under constant pressure to see farther than the predator can, and the predator to close the gap. That instability is the ratchet that drives acuity up.

The control toggle turns predators off. With no food, prey then have no selection pressure at all, so their eyes should drift and stay blind. If they sharpened anyway, the whole predation story would be a lie — the toggle is there to falsify it.
