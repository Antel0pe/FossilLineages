Eyes evolving in a world, not on a fitness curve

No survival formula is written down here. Prey and predators both start blind (a flat light/dark patch), move around a 2D world, and breed by eating — prey eat food, predators eat prey. The only thing an eye does is let you detect things farther away (range = width / Δρ). Whether that's worth the metabolic cost is decided by the chase and the forage, not by me. Watch both eyes sharpen — and watch the arms race between them.

The arms race — mean eye blur Δρ over time

Lower = sharper eye. Both lines should fall as eyes evolve; the predator line chasing the prey line down (or vice-versa) is the arms race. Flat/high = blind.

What is and isn't authored here (read me)

Authored (the substrate): the optics equation Δρ=√((A/f·(1−lens))²+(λ/A)²) shared with the other two tabs; the fact that a bigger/deeper/lensed eye costs more energy per tick (tissue term); fixed movement speeds; and the hard-coded behaviors — prey flee the nearest visible predator, else head to the nearest visible food, else wander; predators pursue the nearest visible prey. Evolvable brains that would let behavior itself emerge are a later step.

NOT authored (it emerges): who survives, who breeds, whether an eye is "worth it," and how sharp each side's eye becomes. There is no survival-probability curve. A prey lives because it happened to spot a predator far enough away to slip out of its sight before contact, and breeds because it ate enough food; a predator breeds because it caught enough prey. Sharper predator eye → larger detection range → fewer prey escape → pressure on prey to detect earlier → arms race. The only reason eyes don't max out instantly is that the tissue costs energy that must be paid back by eating.

The control toggle turns predators off. Prey should then sharpen their eyes only as much as finding food rewards — a weaker, different pull than predation. If eyes sharpened the same with predators off, the predation story would be a lie; the toggle is there to falsify it.
