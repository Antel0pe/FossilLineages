## Atoms, molecules, bonds   [depth: solid]

Atoms are the basic materials — carbon, hydrogen, oxygen. Molecules are atoms bonded
together, like CO₂. Bonded means they share electrons and stick together.

Different atoms pull on shared electrons with different strengths. Oxygen pulls
extremely hard. Carbon and hydrogen pull weakly.

---

## Effective nuclear charge   [depth: sketch]

The pull an electron actually feels is the nucleus's protons minus the shielding from
electrons sitting between it and the nucleus.

Oxygen has 8 protons and 8 electrons — 2 in the first level, 6 in the second. The 2
inner electrons each cancel one proton's worth of pull, so the 6 outer electrons feel
roughly +6 rather than +8. By the same logic carbon's outer electrons feel about +4,
and hydrogen's single electron feels +1.

An electron sitting in a strong positive pull is "comfy" — low energy. One sitting in a
weak pull is uncomfy — high energy. This is the number that ends up mattering for
photosynthesis: oxygen digs a deep valley, carbon digs a shallow one.

## Orbitals   [depth: sketch]

Electrons around a nucleus can only occupy certain wave shapes. Each allowed shape is
an orbital, and each orbital holds 2 electrons — they can only share it if they have
opposite spin.

Level 1 has 1 possible shape, a sphere. Level 2 has 4: a sphere plus up-down,
front-back, and left-right.

## Why strong pull means low energy   [depth: working]

It's like gravity. A ball at the bottom of a deep valley is at lower energy than one in
a shallow dip higher up. Oxygen's +6 is a deep valley; carbon's +4 is a shallow one.

## Redox potential   [depth: working]

How badly something wants electrons, measured as a voltage. Positive voltage means a
terrible donor (it wants to keep them). Negative means a generous donor.
