SOURCES REGISTRY — Horse (Equidae) lineage panels
Deduplicated list of every citation used across 01-05. Numbering matches the numbering used
inside each panel file (a given source keeps the same number everywhere it appears).

1. Zachos, J.C., Pagani, M., Sloan, L., Thomas, E., Billups, K. (2001). "Trends, Rhythms, and
   Aberrations in Global Climate 65 Ma to Present." Science 292(5517):686-693.
   DOI: 10.1126/science.1059412
   Used in: Panel 1 (why-of-the-why — global benthic oxygen-isotope record of the
   Eocene-Oligocene cooling transition).

2. Zanazzi, A., Kohn, M.J., MacFadden, B.J., Terry, D.O. (2007). "Large temperature drop
   across the Eocene-Oligocene transition in central North America." Nature 445:639-642.
   DOI: 10.1038/nature05551
   Used in: Panel 1 (why-of-the-why — independent terrestrial isotope proxy for the same
   transition, from the specific region horses lived in).

3. MacFadden, B.J. (1986). "Fossil horses from 'Eohippus' (Hyracotherium) to Equus: scaling,
   Cope's Law, and the evolution of body size." Paleobiology 12(4):355-369.
   Used in: Panel 1 (baseline — body size stasis through ~25 Ma), Panel 4 (baseline — body
   mass diversification by ~10 Ma).

4. MacFadden, B.J. (1988). "Fossil horses from 'Eohippus' (Hyracotherium) to Equus, 2: rates
   of dental evolution revisited." Biological Journal of the Linnean Society 35(1):37-48.
   DOI: 10.1111/j.1095-8312.1988.tb00457.x
   Used in: Panel 1 (what changed — dental evolution rates/crown height data).

5. Florida Museum of Natural History. "Mesohippus." Fossil Horses in Cyberspace gallery.
   https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/fossil-horses/gallery/mesohippus/
   Used in: Panel 1 (what changed — toe count, leg proportions, premolar molarization).

6. Florida Museum of Natural History. "Parahippus." Fossil Horses in Cyberspace gallery.
   https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/fossil-horses/gallery/parahippus/
   Used in: Panel 2 (what changed — mesodont tooth description).

7. Florida Museum of Natural History. "Merychippus." Fossil Horses in Cyberspace gallery.
   https://www.floridamuseum.ufl.edu/fossil-horses/gallery/merychippus/
   Used in: Panel 3 (baseline — Merychippus tooth/foot description).

8. MacFadden, B.J. (1998). "Equidae." In Janis, C.M., Scott, K.M., Jacobs, L.L. (eds.),
   Evolution of Tertiary Mammals of North America, Volume 1: Terrestrial Carnivores,
   Ungulates, and Ungulatelike Mammals. Cambridge University Press, pp. 537-559.
   Used in: Panel 2 (baseline taxonomy of Miohippus/Anchitheriinae; confidence line on
   resolution of early equid phylogeny).

9. Kaiser, T.M. (2009). "Anchitherium aurelianense (Equidae, Mammalia): a brachydont 'dirty
   browser' in the community of herbivorous large mammals from Sandelzhausen (Miocene,
   Germany)." Paläontologische Zeitschrift 83:131-140. DOI: 10.1007/s12542-009-0002-z
   Used in: Panel 2 (what changed/why-of-the-why — direct Eurasian fossil-community evidence
   Anchitherium stayed a browser after dispersal).

10. Retallack, G.J. (1997). "Neogene expansion of the North American prairie." PALAIOS
    12(4):380-390.
    Used in: Panel 2 (why-of-the-why — paleosol staging of Great Plains grassland opening),
    Panel 4 (why-of-the-why — fully open/arid grassland only from ~6 Ma).

11. Retallack, G.J. (2007). "Cenozoic paleoclimate on land in North America." Journal of
    Geology 115(3):271-294.
    Used in: Panel 2 (why-of-the-why, corroborating source), Panel 4 (why-of-the-why,
    corroborating source).

12. MacFadden, B.J., Hulbert, R.C. (1988). "Explosive speciation at the base of the adaptive
    radiation of Miocene grazing horses." Nature 336:466-468.
    https://www.nature.com/articles/336466a0
    Used in: Panel 3 (baseline — radiation rate, species counts, diversity plateau).

13. Mihlbachler, M.C., Rivals, F., Solounias, N., Semprebon, G.M. (2011). "Dietary Change and
    Evolution of Horses in North America." Science 331(6021):1178-1181.
    DOI: 10.1126/science.1196166
    Used in: Panel 3 (why-of-the-why — mesowear across the full horse fossil record).

14. Jardine, P.E., Janis, C.M., Sahney, S., Benton, M.J. (2012). "Grit not grass: Concordant
    patterns of early origin of hypsodonty in Great Plains ungulates and Glires."
    Palaeogeography, Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology 365:1-10.
    DOI: 10.1016/j.palaeo.2012.09.001
    Used in: Panel 3 (why-of-the-why — grit vs. grass phytoliths as hypsodonty driver).

15. Cerling, T.E., Harris, J.M., MacFadden, B.J., Leakey, M.G., Quade, J., Eisenmann, V.,
    Ehleringer, J.R. (1997). "Global vegetation change through the Miocene/Pliocene
    boundary." Nature 389:153-158. DOI: 10.1038/38229
    Used in: Panel 3 (why-of-the-why — global C4 grass expansion dated 8-6 Ma, postdating
    initial hypsodonty), Panel 4 (why-of-the-why — same C4 expansion signal in the later
    monodactyly/aridification window).

16. Fox, D.L., Koch, P.L. (2003). "Tertiary history of C4 biomass in the Great Plains, USA."
    Geology 31(9):809-812. DOI: 10.1130/G19580.1
    Used in: Panel 4 (why-of-the-why — regional Great Plains paleosol-carbonate C4 biomass
    record, rising sharply 6.4-4.0 Ma).

17. Janis, C.M., Bernor, R.L. (2019). "The Evolution of Equid Monodactyly: A Review Including
    a New Hypothesis." Frontiers in Ecology and Evolution 7:119.
    DOI: 10.3389/fevo.2019.00119
    Used in: Panel 4 (what changed/why — spring-foot elastic energy storage hypothesis for
    monodactyly).

18. Bernor, R.L., Kaya, F., Kaakinen, A., Saarinen, J., Fortelius, M. (2021). "Old World
    hipparion evolution, biogeography, climatology and ecology." Earth-Science Reviews
    221:103784. DOI: 10.1016/j.earscirev.2021.103784
    Used in: Panel 3 (what changed/why — hipparionine dispersal and Old World diversity),
    Panel 5 (baseline/what changed/terminal fact — dispersal, diversity, diet flexibility,
    peak diversity and decline timing).

19. Parker, A.K., McHorse, B.K., Pierce, S.E. (2018). "Niche modeling reveals lack of
    broad-scale habitat partitioning in extinct horses of North America." Palaeogeography,
    Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology 511:103-118.
    https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0031018218301111
    Used in: Panel 5 (why-of-the-why — niche-modeling evidence complicating the trait-to-
    habitat competition narrative).

20. MacFadden, B.J. (2005). "Fossil Horses--Evidence for Evolution." Science
    307(5716):1728-1730. DOI: 10.1126/science.1105458
    General overview source consulted for cross-checking numbers/timeline across all panels;
    not directly quoted as a specific claim citation in any panel above.

27. Prothero, D.R., Shubin, N. (1989). "The evolution of Oligocene horses." In Prothero, D.R.,
    Schoch, R.M. (eds.), The Evolution of Perissodactyls. Oxford University Press, pp. 142-175.
    Used in: Panel 6 (what changed/why-of-the-why — cladogenetic origin of Miohippus from
    Mesohippus, ~4 Myr stratigraphic overlap at shared sites, body size/facial fossa
    differences, repeated stasis-then-branching pattern across other contemporaneous equid
    lineages).

28. MacFadden, B.J. (1992). Fossil Horses: Systematics, Paleobiology, and Evolution of the

    Used in: Panel 6 (what changed — general reference for Mesohippus/Miohippus body size and
    morphological comparison).

29. Janis, C.M., Damuth, J., Theodor, J.M. (2000). "Miocene ungulates and terrestrial primary
    productivity: Where have all the browsers gone?" Proceedings of the National Academy of
    Sciences 97(14):7899-7904. DOI: 10.1073/pnas.97.14.7899
    Used in: Panel 7 (why/why-of-the-why — 37-locality quantitative decline in browser species
    richness ~19-10 Ma, proposed CO2/primary-productivity mechanism, explicit finding that the
    decline was not a one-for-one replacement by grazers).

30. Janis, C.M., Damuth, J., Theodor, J.M. (2002). "The origins and evolution of the North

    Palaeoclimatology, Palaeoecology 177(1-2):183-198. DOI: 10.1016/S0031-0182(01)00359-5
    Used in: Panel 7 (baseline/terminal fact — Hypohippus and Megahippus persisting through
    the Clarendonian chronofauna and going extinct at its close).

31. Heintzman, P.D., Zazula, G.D., MacPhee, R.D.E., Scott, E., Cahill, J.A., McHorse, B.K.,
